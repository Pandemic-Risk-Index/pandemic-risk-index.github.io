Discussion paper · October 2021

UK SMEs: quantifying their pandemic risk and credit risk exposures in the wake of the COVID-19 crisis

Ahmed Barakat, Meryem Duygun (Principal Investigator), Maurizio Fiaschetti, Eddie Gerba, Tian Han, Enrico Onali, Afshin Sabri, Mike Tsionas and Huamao Wang

This discussion paper on the Pandemic Risk Index (PRI) was developed in collaboration with the Bank of England, the Confederation of British Industry (CBI), Experian and partner universities: the University of Nottingham, the University of Bristol, Lancaster University, University College London and the London School of Economics. The project was funded by UK Research and Innovation (UKRI), grant number ES/V015419/1.

About the paper

COVID-19 lockdowns put small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) at particular risk of failure, and UK SMEs were among the hardest hit across OECD countries. This paper builds a Pandemic Risk Index (PRI) framework that condenses firm-level, regional, sectoral and national information into a small set of indices, tests how well they predict UK firms' post-COVID solvency, and applies machine learning, network analysis and clustering to SME credit assessment.

The analysis covers 218,858 UK firms, combining their accounts from FAME (Bureau van Dijk) with ONS survey data, UK government COVID-19 case data, stock returns from Capital IQ and the Oxford COVID-19 Government Response Tracker.

Firm-level indices

Financial, non-financial and overall indices, built by principal component analysis from firms' pre-COVID accounts and characteristics.

Regional Covid Social Index

A composite of ONS survey measures of COVID's social impact on households: anxiety, self-isolation, pessimism, affordability and access to information.

Sectoral Covid Exposure Index

Ranks 66 sectors by their stock-market reaction on 16–17 March 2020, when the Prime Minister announced the first COVID-19 restrictions.

Machine-learning credit assessment

Models estimated for the 12 UK regions, plus network analysis and K-means clustering to identify firms with similar credit profiles.

Key findings

  • The firm-level indices built from pre-COVID accounts are significant predictors of both post-COVID credit scores and County Court Judgements against firms.
  • Firms in the sectors hit hardest by the March 2020 lockdown news have lower post-COVID credit scores and larger County Court Judgements, even after accounting for firm-level and regional factors.
  • In regions where COVID-19 hit households harder, with more anxiety, self-isolation and pessimism, firms faced larger County Court Judgements: about 26% higher per standard deviation of the Regional Covid Social Index.
  • A firm's post-COVID credit score depends not only on its own pre-COVID score but also on those of peers with similar credit profiles, identified through network analysis and K-means clustering.
  • Credit-score distributions differ by region: London's is fairly even, while most other regions are polarised between very low and very high scores.
